Music X-Ray

Seven ways to show what Magic Music Visuals can hear in a track, from the audio alone. Each panel is a concept to comment on. All of them run on one simulated 126 BPM track in A minor with an intro, two buildups, two drops and a breakdown, so you can see every detector react. Press Sound to hear the track the visuals are following.

A

Beat Orbit

The bar is an orbit and the planet is "now". Each kick nudges the planet back onto its beat marker; between kicks it keeps going on its own. When the kick stops, the orbit turns dashed: the clock is free-running on its last tempo.

The big marker is bar 1. Live audio cannot know where bar 1 is, so it is a guess until the first drop sets it. The outer ring counts bars since the last drop.

B

Spectral Terrain

The spectrum as a landscape that flows towards you. Left is low (sub-bass, kick), right is high (hats, air). Watch the left hills vanish in the breakdown and the right side rise during a buildup.

Labels pop up over the hill that each instrument makes, so you learn where a kick or a hat lives in the frequency range.

C

Song River

The track so far as a river: width is loudness, colour is what the live detector thinks is happening. Pins mark the moments it noticed, with the reason. The thin strip on top is the real structure, which a live detector never sees ahead of time.

Compare the strip with the river to see where live detection lags (a buildup is only certain after about two bars) and where it is right on the beat (the drop).

D

Drop Radar

Shows the detector's reasoning. Three cues build tension: the sub-bass has been out for bars, the sound gets brighter, the snare roll gets denser. When the sub-bass comes back on a bar line, that is the drop.

Tuned on the user's 189 tracks: the sub-bass step (0.2 to 0.76) is the most reliable drop cue. A silent kick before the drop happens in fewer than half of them.

E

Who's Playing

One shape per instrument, placed where you would feel it: the kick as a heart, the sub-bass as the floor, the snare as a burst, the hats as sparks overhead. Each shape lists the frequency range its detector listens to.

Counters show how many hits each detector caught, so you can see the snare roll speed up in a buildup.

F

Note Wheel

The twelve notes around a circle. A trail follows the melody, and petals grow for notes that come back often, which reveals the key. In the breakdown the lead plays alone and the trail is clean. In a drop the bass and drums confuse the pitch detector, and the wheel shows that as scatter.

The glow around the wheel is brightness (Tone), the cue that rises through a buildup.

G

Tempo Lens

How tempo is found: the time between kicks, collected into a histogram. The tall bar is the beat. Snare-roll hits in a buildup add short intervals at half the beat.

Every interval becomes a BPM value and is doubled or halved until it lands in one tempo octave (here 90–180). That folding fixes half- and double-time readings. Pick the octave to match the set.

KickPulseDtExpression: interval, fold
